CONTENT DESIGN / STATUS LANGUAGE

Overview

The interface needed to say what the system was actually doing.

I tested complete owner and sitter journeys and found places where labels, actions, and system behavior contradicted one another. My role went beyond proofreading: I identified the user’s question, clarified the real state, wrote the next safe action, and converted important content expectations into QA checks.

01

STATUS LANGUAGE

Separate what the owner did from what the sitter still needed to do.

After an owner accepted a sitter, the interface announced Booking Confirmed before the sitter had confirmed. The copy promised more certainty than the system could support.

When confirmation is pendingBooking ConfirmedWaiting for sitter confirmation

Names the remaining actor instead of implying the booking is complete.

When the invitation is declinedBooking ConfirmedInvitation declined

Removes stale booking actions and offers “Find another sitter.”

02

ACTION LABELS

Two opposite outcomes should never share the same word.

In the cancellation dialog, Cancel dismissed the warning while Cancel Booking performed the destructive action. Under pressure, that distinction was too easy to miss.

03

EMPTY STATES + MICROCOPY

Replace database language with useful invitations.

Small strings shaped whether the product felt empty, broken, or ready for participation. I rewrote them to describe the present state and make the next action visible.

04

SIGN-IN + RECOGNITION

Help returning users recognize the right path.

The sign-in flow asked for an email without acknowledging recently used accounts. I kept the flexible entry point and added recognition and recovery language.

05

HUMAN SUPPORT

When the user asks for a person, confirm the handoff.

Recommended response
I can connect you with Nuzzly Support. Your conversation summary will be included so you won’t need to repeat everything.
Message Nuzzly Support

The response confirms the destination, explains what transfers, and presents one action.

06

TRANSPARENCY + CONTENT GOVERNANCE

Explain when private information becomes available.

The original dashboard provided no explanation of when an owner’s contact details would be shared. A later product state added an Emergency & support panel and made the confirmation boundary visible. That was meaningful progress, even though the sentence still exposed backend terminology.

Outcome

A reusable language system—not a collection of one-off fixes.

I documented more than 40 content and UX issues and created patterns for status, actions, empty states, sign-in, support, privacy, and recovery. These became testable expectations for future releases.

Original screens document the states I reviewed. “After” visuals are redesign concepts based on my recommendations, not presented as shipped production work. No unverified performance metric is claimed.
