Designing the marketplace beyond mobile-sized screens.

My work covered the public website and browser-based owner and sitter journeys. The desktop search entry point and mobile booking state shown above represent different parts of an experience that needed consistent terminology, actions, and trust cues.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Why I chose coral.

Coral feels warm, friendly, and energetic—qualities that fit a community built around pets, homes, and personal trust. I reserved it for primary actions, active states, and moments needing attention, while dark neutrals and light surfaces preserved hierarchy.

MESSAGING + BOOKING STATES

Turn conversation into a dependable transaction record.

Direct messages carried applications, owner acceptance, sitter confirmation, date changes, cancellations, and safety communication. A card could still say Booking Confirmed after the timeline said the sitter declined. My redesign makes the status, explanation, and available action change together, with Find another sitter as the relevant next step.

DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONS

Make opposite choices look and sound different.

The cancellation dialog used “Cancel” to keep a booking and “Cancel Booking” to end it. I changed the safe action to Keep booking, retained an explicit destructive label, and gave the two actions distinct visual weight.

03

DASHBOARD + LISTING MANAGEMENT

Organize each role around its next decision.

Owners needed listing, trip, application, and review actions; sitters needed application and booking updates. Both roles needed reliable status, direct messages, and support. The later dashboard exposed technical contact-sharing copy and duplicated messaging, so I proposed a clearer next-action hierarchy and simpler language.

COPY EVOLUTION

01 / BEFORE

No contact-sharing explanation

Users could not tell when the owner’s private contact details would become available.

02 / LATER PRODUCT STATE

“Owner private contact details are hidden until the backend shares confirmed-sit support fields.”

A rule appeared, but its wording described the implementation instead of what users needed to know.

03 / MY RECOMMENDATION

“Contact details will be shared after confirmation.”

Keep the confirmation rule; say it in plain language.

OWNER ACTION

Add trip

Surface a frequent owner task alongside Create listing and Find a sitter.

EMPTY-STATE ACCURACY

No active sits right now

Avoid “yet” when the person may have completed stays before.

ATTENTION HIERARCHY

Show workload

Bring applications, unread messages, and pending reviews out of hidden tabs.

INTERACTION CONSISTENCY

One message action

Keep the action attached to the right listing or booking.

SUPPORTING EXAMPLE · SUPPORT CHAT

Complete the human handoff.

When a user agreed to connect with support, the system needed to acknowledge the request, explain what would transfer, and provide one clear next action.
