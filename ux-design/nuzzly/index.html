OVERVIEW

A two-sided marketplace can’t work when users don’t know what state they’re in.

Nuzzly connects pet owners with sitters. I tested complete journeys across discovery, applications, booking, messaging, support, and listing management. The main problem wasn’t one isolated screen: the experience communicated different rules in different places.

I mapped the breakdowns, redesigned the highest-risk flows, and paired interaction changes with language that accurately reflected system behavior.

01

EXPLORE + DISCOVERY

Make the user’s goal visible before showing the inventory.

The original Explore experience mixed search modes, location filters, and saved-item behavior. Before users could evaluate a listing, they first had to interpret how the page worked.

I reorganized the experience around the primary task—Find a sit—then clarified filter labels, separated view controls from saved items, and strengthened the information hierarchy of each result.

OWNER LISTING JOURNEY

Complete verification→Get membership→Create listing→Publish→Review applications

SITTER FILTERS

Keep applied criteria visible and easy to revise.

Filters appeared after search without a compact summary, removable selections, or a clear reset action. The redesign uses chips, Filters 2, Clear all, result count, and sorting so the current search state never disappears.

02

MESSAGING + BOOKING STATES

Design one source of truth for a changing transaction.

After an owner accepted a sitter, the interface could display Booking Confirmed while the timeline said the sitter had declined. Stale buttons remained available, so the next step was unclear.

I redesigned the card as a state-based component: the status, explanation, and action change together. A declined invitation removes invalid booking controls and leads the owner toward finding another sitter.

OWNER ACCEPTS→WAITING FOR SITTER→CONFIRMEDorDECLINED → FIND ANOTHER SITTER

DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONS

Make opposite choices look and sound different.

The cancellation dialog used “Cancel” to keep a booking and “Cancel Booking” to end it. I changed the safe action to Keep booking, retained an explicit destructive label, and gave the two actions distinct visual weight.

03

DASHBOARD + LISTING MANAGEMENT

Show what users can do now—and what becomes available later.

The dashboard mixed applications, active sits, support, and messaging without explaining when private contact details were shared. Some actions appeared twice, while important state boundaries were invisible.

I treated the dashboard as an operational workspace: one primary action per intent, clearer tabs, visible confirmation status, and transparent rules for contact sharing and emergency support.

SUPPORT HANDOFF

Complete the user’s request to reach a person.

When a user wrote “ok connect me,” the system did not acknowledge or initiate the handoff. The revised pattern confirms the destination, explains that the conversation summary transfers, and provides one next action.

I can connect you with Nuzzly Support. Your conversation summary will be included so you won’t need to repeat everything.

OUTCOME

A clearer framework for a trust-sensitive marketplace.

I documented more than 40 UX, content, and product issues, then connected the highest-risk findings to redesigned screens and release checks. The result was a clearer set of patterns for discovery, booking states, privacy, support, and recovery across both marketplace roles.

A booking card, its timeline, and its available actions should tell the same story.
