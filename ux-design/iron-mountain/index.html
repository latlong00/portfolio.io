OVERVIEW

From an asset record to an explorable gallery.

Iron Mountain’s Global Innovation Team was exploring a secure web-based experience for managing and showcasing customer assets. A static record could identify an object, but it could not let someone inspect its shape. During my internship, I proposed a digital gallery where visitors could rotate a 3D model and read about the asset beside it.

I researched the experience, mapped the gallery in Figma, created the 3D models, and built a working WordPress prototype. The gallery was an exploratory proposal, not a launched product feature.

01

GENERATIVE RESEARCH

Find the right path from stored asset to 3D display.

I interviewed stakeholders and studied comparable asset and gallery experiences to understand both the business constraints and the visitor’s task. I also worked through the model-to-embed process myself, because the idea depended on a practical way to publish interactive objects.

That work separated two needs: contributors needed a reliable route from model to published page; visitors needed enough context to know what they were viewing and where to explore next. I used those needs to shape the gallery navigation and asset detail view.

DESIGN DIRECTION

Bring the asset, its context, and the next step into one understandable flow: manage it securely, preview it, then prepare it to share.

02

PROTOTYPING + 3D EXPLORATION

Make the gallery concept tangible.

In Figma, I mapped the gallery structure and interactions: collection navigation, a featured model viewer, object details, and related assets. I shared early concepts with designers, engineers, and product managers to discuss what the experience needed before building it.

I then created models in SketchUp, hosted them on Sketchfab for embedding, and built the digital gallery in WordPress. The visual below reconstructs the interface direction; the underlying internship work remains under NDA.

03

COLLABORATION + PROPOSAL

Show how the concept could work.

I walked stakeholders through the journey from a managed asset to a model visitors could inspect in the gallery. Showing a working prototype gave the team a way to discuss the design, publishing steps, and business value together.

My original case study reports an approximately 40% reduction in integration time.

REFLECTION

What stayed with me.

Building the models and the WordPress gallery made technical constraints visible while there was still time to change the design. Showing that working concept helped turn research into a discussion across disciplines.

I had the privilege of working with some of the most talented and kind-hearted people during my internship. A special thank you to Melisa Harder, Will Allen, Rita Stern, Gabriel Nguyen, and everyone else on Iron Mountain’s Innovation Team for giving me this amazing opportunity!
