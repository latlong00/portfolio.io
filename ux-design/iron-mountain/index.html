OVERVIEW

From protected assets to a usable web experience.

Iron Mountain helps organizations protect and manage valuable information and assets. During summer 2022, I joined its Global Innovation Team as a UX Research Intern to explore a secure, web-based SaaS platform where customers could manage and showcase their assets.

My work covered the path from discovery to proposal: generative research, competitor analysis, stakeholder interviews, design exploration, interactive prototypes in Figma, SketchUp, and WordPress, and a presentation to key stakeholders. I collaborated with product managers, designers, and engineers across research, design, and execution.

GENERATIVE RESEARCH

Understand the assets, the workflow, and the people involved.

The challenge was broader than designing a single screen: the team needed a coherent way to move from a protected customer asset to something a person could inspect and share. I interviewed stakeholders to learn the business goals and constraints, reviewed competing experiences to understand patterns for asset organization and preview, and worked through the creation-to-publishing flow myself.

3D modeling was new to me. I learned the tools directly and asked cross-functional partners for feedback, so the design discussion could account for what was feasible as well as what would help customers. PROTOTYPING + 3D EXPLORATION

Use the prototype to make an unfamiliar idea tangible.

I explored how 3D models could make an asset more tangible in the web experience. SketchUp let me create and prepare a model, Figma helped me structure the interface and interaction flow, and WordPress let me test a publishing context. Together, those tools helped me move from an abstract idea to an interactive prototype.

I shared low-fidelity explorations early with product managers, engineers, and designers. Those conversations made questions about page hierarchy, preview behavior, and feasibility easier to address before polishing the concept. COLLABORATION + PROPOSAL

Bring the team into the idea before asking them to approve it.

The final presentation brought the research, proposed experience, and business case together for key stakeholders. I used the interactive prototype to show how an asset could move through the experience and why that approach might be valuable, giving the team a concrete starting point for future initiatives.

Across the internship, I contributed to an end-to-end flow within three months. TAKEAWAYS

What I carried forward from the project.

01

Bring visuals to early conversations.

Bringing visuals is helpful when sharing initial ideas with cross-functional partners. By sharing low-fidelity explorations and visual references, I could better convey ideas to engineers, product managers, and UX designers.

02

Translate data into designs.

Converting complex data and feedback into actionable design strategies was a critical part of my role. This experience taught me the importance of clear communication and the ability to present research findings in a way that stakeholders can easily understand and act upon.

03

Be proactive, creative, and bold.

At first, it was challenging to explore an area that I had not explored before: 3D modeling. I researched online and reached out to cross-functional partners in my team to gather insights and feedback. Through this process, I was able to create new interactions through 3D models, bringing new perspective to what the team can do with Iron Mountain’s assets.

REFLECTION

Iteration, collaboration, and gratitude.

My time at Iron Mountain reinforced the value of iterative design and continuous improvement. User feedback and data analysis were integral to refining the platform to meet changing needs. This approach is something I carry forward in all my projects, always striving for better iterations.

Being part of the Innovation Team and working on this project was incredibly enriching and valuable, especially in learning how to collaborate effectively with a wide range of roles, make informed design decisions as a team, and present work to colleagues and stakeholders.

I had the privilege of working with some of the most talented and kind-hearted people during my internship. A special thank you to Melisa Harder, Will Allen, Rita Stern, Gabriel Nguyen, and everyone else on Iron Mountain’s Innovation Team for giving me this amazing opportunity!
