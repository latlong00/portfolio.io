OVERVIEW

A place to manage assets—and experience them.

In summer 2022, I joined Iron Mountain’s Global Innovation Team as a UX Research Intern. The project explored a secure, web-based experience for managing customer assets. I designed and built a WordPress gallery prototype to showcase assets in 3D. The viewing experience worked like a digital gallery, supported by an asset-management workflow.

I researched the problem, designed the gallery in Figma, created and hosted 3D models, built the WordPress experience, and presented the proposal to stakeholders with product managers, designers, and engineers. The project is under NDA, so the visuals here explain my process rather than show confidential product screens.

01

GENERATIVE RESEARCH

Find the right path from stored asset to 3D display.

I interviewed stakeholders, studied comparable experiences, and tested the steps for creating and publishing a 3D model. This helped me understand how an asset could move from storage and management into an explorable online presentation.

Because 3D modeling was new to me, I worked through the tools myself and used early visuals to discuss feasibility with engineers, designers, and product managers.

STAKEHOLDER INTERVIEWS

Understand the business context

I gathered needs, constraints, and expectations from partners across the Innovation Team.

COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Study existing interaction patterns

I compared how adjacent tools organize assets, permissions, previews, and publishing.

WORKFLOW TESTING

Learn through direct use

I worked through the model-to-embed workflow while building the gallery.

Model→Preview→Publish
DESIGN DIRECTION

Bring the asset, its context, and the next step into one understandable flow: manage it securely, preview it, then prepare it to share.

02

PROTOTYPING + 3D EXPLORATION

Make the gallery concept tangible.

I created the 3D models in SketchUp and hosted them on Sketchfab so I could embed them in WordPress. I mapped the overall digital gallery design in Figma, then built the gallery in WordPress with the embedded models.

Sharing rough visuals early helped the team discuss the experience and technical constraints before I refined the proposal.

03

COLLABORATION + PROPOSAL

Show how the concept could work.

I presented the research, interactive prototype, and business case to key stakeholders. Showing the asset journey gave the team a concrete starting point for future initiatives.

Within the three-month internship, I helped develop an end-to-end flow. My original case study reports an approximately 40% reduction in integration time; implementation details remain under NDA.

TAKEAWAYS

What I carried forward from the project.

01

Bring visuals to early conversations.

Low-fidelity visuals gave engineers, product managers, and designers something concrete to discuss before the concept was polished.

02

Translate data into designs.

Research was most useful when I translated it into a flow and prototype that stakeholders could evaluate.

03

Be proactive, creative, and bold.

Learning 3D modeling and asking for feedback helped me test an unfamiliar interaction idea.

REFLECTION

What stayed with me.

The project taught me to make unfamiliar ideas visible early, gather feedback across disciplines, and connect a prototype to a clear proposal.
