OVERVIEW

Giving customer assets a more useful way to be seen.

In summer 2022, I joined Iron Mountain’s Global Innovation Team as a UX Research Intern. The team was exploring a secure web-based platform for managing and showcasing customer assets. My project asked how a customer could go beyond an asset record and inspect the object itself.

Over three months, I researched, designed, and built a digital gallery prototype with interactive 3D models. I presented it as a business proposal to stakeholders. The work was exploratory and remains under NDA.

01

GENERATIVE RESEARCH

Find the right path from stored asset to 3D display.

I conducted stakeholder interviews, generative research, and competitor analysis to understand what the platform needed to support. I brought early visual references to conversations with product managers, designers, and engineers so we could discuss the concept before committing to a build.

The research pointed to a gallery experience that paired an explorable object with its details and a clear path through the collection. It also raised a practical question: how could a 3D model reach a web page that someone could actually use?

02

PROTOTYPING + 3D EXPLORATION

Make the gallery concept tangible.

I used Figma to map the overall gallery design and interactions, including collection navigation, the model viewer, and asset details. I created the 3D models in SketchUp, hosted them on Sketchfab so they could be embedded, and built the complete digital gallery in WordPress.

The interface below is a reconstruction of that design direction for this portfolio; the original internship screens are under NDA.

03

PROPOSAL + IMPACT

A working concept to discuss with stakeholders.

I presented the end-to-end prototype as a business proposal, showing how an asset could become an interactive gallery entry. The working build helped stakeholders evaluate the experience and the steps needed to produce it. My original internship case study reports that this approach reduced integration time by approximately 40% and gave the team a starting point for future initiatives.

REFLECTION

What stayed with me.

3D modeling was new to me. Learning it meant researching the tools and asking teammates for feedback as the concept took shape. Bringing visuals into those conversations helped me translate research into design decisions people across disciplines could respond to.

I had the privilege of working with some of the most talented and kind-hearted people during my internship. A special thank you to Melisa Harder, Will Allen, Rita Stern, Gabriel Nguyen, and everyone else on Iron Mountain’s Innovation Team for giving me this amazing opportunity!
